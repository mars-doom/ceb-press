The machine — ~$500 core

A stock towable 25-ton log splitter IS the press: beam = frame, cylinder = ram, engine + two-stage pump + valve + reservoir = the power pack. One fabricated part — the mold box — bolts on with zero alteration (unbolts back to a working log splitter). 8×12×4″ jumbo bricks at ~520 psi face pressure; standard bricks for vault bands.

Robotics path — scrounged, ~$300 entry

  • Teleop earthwork — Chicago Electric 3,000 lb winch w/ wireless remote (~$150, found on Craigslist) + slip pan + deadman anchor: the remote is the teleop. Same winch remounts on a monorail as the brick hoist.
  • Press auto-cycle — wiper motor + cam + limit switches on the valve lever, ~$30–80 scrounged.
  • Brick-placement gantry — Cartesian rail over the wall, ~$1–3k DIY. Our leaned-vault method makes this the simplest published-compatible robot task: every course rests on the party wall, no support robot needed (research systems like VaultBot / NASA ARMADAS need cooperative support arms — ours don't, and cost ~1%).
  • Loaders/tractors — demoted to "if a deal lands": Ford 2N $1,000 Avondale, Int'l+loader $2,500 Gardner, JD 1050 $3,500 Pueblo (stale = negotiable).

Mars transfer — verified against literature

  • Dry-compression is a published route — Martian simulant compacts into blocks with no binder/water (Nature Sci. Reports). Our ~520 psi is below published test pressures → MICP is the binder fallback (3.3–5.65 MPa on simulant, survives 1% perchlorate).
  • Gravity: 0.38g scales thrust and confining weight together — Earth is the harder case.
  • Shell ≠ habitat: dry-stack shell is a radiation shield around a pressurized bladder — shielding claims need >1 m regolith (dose peaks at 0.4 m).
  • Teleop ≠ Mars ops: 4–24 min light delay → supervised autonomy is the real architecture; the Earth winch-demo proves the task is remote-buildable.
  • Carbon: Earth envelope can take 4–6% coarse biochar (~8–10 t CO₂e buried/structure) — functional (moisture + acoustic fill), sequestration rides along. Mars substitutes scoria.
