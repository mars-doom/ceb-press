# Log

### 2026.11.15 — The machine question settles

Searched hard for alternatives — used excavator-mounted presses, balers, bottle-jack
builds, a Red Arrow press, fabricating a frame. A towable log splitter beat all of
them: it IS a powered horizontal press (beam + ram + engine + pump + valve + tank,
factory-matched, towable, and unbolts back to a working log splitter). Product sizes
got sized to the stock 25 tons — the 8×12×4 in jumbo brick lands at ~520 psi face
pressure, near the practical ceiling.

### 2026.11.15 — Venue hunt → gatehouse

Started as a dome for a desert venue, became a performance-hall bay, then a single
gatehouse/dromos: open arched mouth, battered rib walls, a Nubian-style vault that
leans ~15° onto the party wall, a rear cell, and acoustic cavities in the ribs
(Greek echea — the structure is also a speaker cabinet).

### 2026.11.16 — The earthwork problem

The honest math killed the deep-berm design: ~130 yd³ of earth sheltering is weeks
of shoveling, not days. The fix — six stepped counterforts (masonry-faced soil
buttresses) carry the vault thrust as positioned mass, haunch banks shrink to ~44 in,
cap thins to ~10 in of armor. ~55 yd³ total. Static FoS actually *improved* (~3.3)
because buttresses beat berms per cubic yard.

### 2026.11.16 — Two real defects found by staring at the mesh

The thinned rear wall left a wedge open to the sky where the vault's last band dips —
fixed with a stepped gable on the rear liner. The trimmed cap still ran ~4 ft past
where the walls end — fixed to feather onto the rear bank crest. Both found by
raycasting the actual STL, not by eyeballing the preview.

### 2026.11.16 — Sourcing sweep

All parts located on Craigslist (see parts list): the $350 Ft Garland splitter, a
wireless-remote winch that doubles as teleoperated earth mover AND brick hoist,
fittings, stove. A rebuildable hydraulic cylinder on hand may become the 35–42T
upgrade ram if the bore measures ≥5 in and the chrome is clean. Nothing purchased yet.
